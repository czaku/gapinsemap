There's a gap
in SE map.

Walworth and central Camberwell sit in zone 1–2 with no rail station of any kind. Two railway lines pass through them without stopping. The proposed Bakerloo extension would fix Old Kent Road and the east — but leaves Walworth and Camberwell as a transport desert. We can do better.

The gap — precisely

It's not "south London". It's a specific pair of postcodes that have nothing.

Be honest about geography. Peckham has rail — Peckham Rye is a major Thameslink and Windrush interchange, and Queen's Road Peckham and Nunhead serve it too. Bermondsey has the Jubilee line. South Bermondsey has its own (low-usage) station and Surrey Quays is a short walk. The "south London transport black hole" everyone talks about is not actually evenly distributed.

The real desert is two adjacent postcodes: SE17 (Walworth) and the centre of SE5 (Camberwell), with the Old Kent Road corridor as a linear extension to the east. From Walworth Road or Camberwell Green, the closest station is a 12–18 minute walk in most directions — Elephant, Kennington, Oval, Loughborough Junction, Denmark Hill, or Peckham Rye. None of them is in the area you live and work in.

And here's the punchline: two operational railways cut straight through this area without stopping. Thameslink trains pass through the old Walworth Road and Camberwell platforms every few minutes on the line from Loughborough Junction to Elephant. London Bridge viaduct trains thunder over north Bermondsey. Both routes used to stop here.

The stations that closed and never came back

  1. Walworth Road — closed 1 April 1916. Platforms still visible from satellite. Thameslink trains pass through every few minutes today.
  2. Camberwell — closed 3 April 1916. Building still standing on Camberwell Station Road, boarded up.
  3. Old Kent Road (a.k.a. Old Kent Road & Hatcham) — closed 1 January 1917.
  4. Spa Road & Bermondsey — closed 15 March 1915. London's first ever railway terminus (1836).
  5. Southwark Park — closed 1 January 1915.

Closed "temporarily" to release coal, steel and railway workers for WW1. Wealthier-area stations reopened. These didn't. The line through Walworth Rd and Camberwell still operates — Thameslink runs over the empty platforms multiple times an hour. It would not be hard to put trains back.

Who lives here

~85,000 residents in Walworth & Camberwell. Hundreds of millions in tax. Zero stations.

Conservative: ~35,000 employed residents × ~£8k average income tax + employee NI (Southwark median salary £58.4k; the gap area sits below the borough median). Plus VAT, stamp duty and business rates from Old Kent Road and Walworth traders.

12,000+
new homes coming on Old Kent Road

The Mayor's London Plan designates Old Kent Road an Opportunity Area for 12,000 new homes and 5,000 jobs by 2041. Southwark has already approved 9,500. The east gets new rail (the BLE). The west gets the homes without the rail.

25,000
homes the Bakerloo extension would unlock — eastward

TfL's own figure. The extension would run Elephant → Burgess Park → Old Kent Rd → New Cross Gate → Lewisham. Welcome — but it leaves Walworth Rd, Camberwell and Denmark Hill without any new station. The 1940s plan to extend through Camberwell was scrapped.

0
train stations in Walworth or central Camberwell

Not "few". Zero. The closest stations — Elephant & Castle, Kennington, Oval, Loughborough Jct, Denmark Hill, Peckham Rye — are 12–25 minute walks from the centre of the desert. Old Kent Road, the only artery, runs 60 buses an hour and is at capacity.

Population, income and tax figures are conservative estimates derived from ONS Census 2021 ward data, Southwark Council ward profiles, the Trust for London poverty data (gross household income £73,800), and the 2026/27 Southwark council tax schedule. Population figure is intentionally narrower than the wider "south-London transport black hole" framing in order to be defensible — Peckham, Nunhead, Bermondsey and Surrey Quays do have rail and are not double-counted here. The challenge

The Bakerloo extension is welcome. It's also only half a plan.

Read the proposed route carefully. Elephant & Castle → Burgess Park (eastern edge) → Old Kent Road → Old Kent Road → New Cross Gate → Lewisham. Every new station is east of Walworth Road. The west half of the gap — Walworth, Camberwell Green, Denmark Hill — gets nothing.

Walworth gets one edge station

Burgess Park is on the eastern fringe of Walworth, ~10 minutes from Walworth Road itself. From the heart of SE17 you'd still walk further to a tube than most people do in zone 4. That's not a fix. That's a consolation prize.

Camberwell gets nothing.

Not one new station. The 1940s plan to extend the Bakerloo to Camberwell got as far as the official tube map and was scrapped. The 2014 reopening study was ignored. The current proposal pretends the entire SE5 desert doesn't exist.

Denmark Hill is overloaded.

Camberwell residents who can walk that far cram into Denmark Hill, which is already a bottleneck on Thameslink, Windrush and Southeastern. Adding 12,000 Old Kent Road homes to the east doesn't relieve it — it makes it worse.

Trains already pass through Walworth Rd & Camberwell.

The Loughborough Junction → Elephant Thameslink line crosses central Camberwell and Walworth Road every few minutes. The platforms still exist. The closures were a 1916 emergency measure. Reopening them is one of the cheapest possible rail wins anywhere in inner London.

The "west arm" was always supposed to exist.

A Bakerloo branch through Walworth Rd → Camberwell to Brixton (Victoria line interchange) was on tube maps in the 1930s and 40s. Every decade since has produced a new study and zero metal. Brixton terminus = Bakerloo-to-Victoria interchange in one step — the kind of network upgrade south London hasn't had in a generation.

Bottom line.

We support the Bakerloo extension. We do not accept it as the whole answer. Half the gap is still the gap.

The plan

Four asks. The cheap one first.

Two of these have already been studied by TfL. One is already approved and funded. The fourth is the long-term ambition the city has been ducking for ninety years. None of them are radical.

  1. 01 Reopen Walworth Road and Camberwell stations. Now.

    Closed in 1916. The line still operates — Thameslink trains pass through every few minutes without stopping. TfL's 2014 feasibility study costed reopening at ≤£50m (Walworth Rd) and ≤£30m (Camberwell). Today: ~£100m + ~£60m. Together, roughly two weeks of HS2 contingency. Add both to the next New Stations Fund round and deliver before 2030. This is the quickest win in inner London — line built, platforms half there, trains already running.

  2. 02 Plan the Bakerloo "west arm" — Elephant to Brixton.

    Elephant & Castle → Walworth Road → Camberwell Green → Loughborough Junction → Brixton. Brixton is the killer terminus: it creates the only new tube-to-tube interchange south of the river since the Jubilee extension — Bakerloo to Victoria line in one step. That's a network multiplier, not just a local fix. (Denmark Hill, Streatham or Crystal Palace remain options for further extension; Brixton is the threshold ask.) The 1940s plan to extend the Bakerloo to Camberwell appeared on the official tube map and was scrapped without serious rebuttal. Every BLE iteration since has dropped the western branch — without ever explaining why Camberwell's 85,000 residents matter less than Lewisham's. Reinstate the western arm in the next BLE feasibility revision and formally cost a Brixton terminus.

  3. 03 Fund the Bakerloo east arm. This Spending Review.

    Elephant → Burgess Park → Old Kent Road (×2) → New Cross Gate → Lewisham. We back this. 89% of 2019 consultation respondents supported it. Feasibility design is ongoing. The Treasury must commit a multi-year capital settlement that explicitly names BLE. Target: construction by 2030, service by 2035.

  4. 04 Build Surrey Canal Road overground station — on time.

    Lewisham Council has approved funding. £25m secured from developers. TfL says work could start in 2026 and finish by 2028. Don't let it slip. Lowest hanging fruit and serves the eastern flank.

Why this order matters. Politicians regularly use a moonshot ask (a tube branch) as cover for delivering nothing. By leading with the cheap, achievable reopening of Walworth Road and Camberwell — which the line and platforms can already support — we make refusal indefensible. The west arm is the long game; the reopenings are the test of good faith.

Interim demands

While the Mayor fails his statutory duty, we still need to move.

Reopening stations and building tunnels takes years. The non-compliance is happening now — and so is the cost to residents. Every day Walworth and Camberwell go without rail, we're paying out of pocket for substitution: Ubers, Limes, Forest bikes, taxis. We want that recognised — and underwritten — until the rail arrives.

The provisions being missed

Greater London Authority Act 1999, section 141(1)

"The Mayor shall develop and implement policies for the promotion and encouragement of safe, integrated, efficient and economic transport facilities and services to, from and within Greater London."

Failure: Walworth and central Camberwell — zone 1–2, ~85,000 residents — have no rail facility at all. That is not "integrated".

Greater London Authority Act 1999, section 142(2)

The Transport Strategy "shall contain the Mayor's proposals for the provision of transport which is accessible to persons with mobility problems," and shall specify "a timetable for the implementation of those proposals".

Failure: No timetable has been set for restoring rail access to Walworth or Camberwell. The 1940s plan was scrapped. The 2014 reopening study was shelved. The current BLE proposal explicitly excludes the western half of the gap.

Equality Act 2010, section 149(1) — Public Sector Equality Duty

"A public authority must, in the exercise of its functions, have due regard to the need to … advance equality of opportunity between persons who share a relevant protected characteristic and persons who do not share it…"

Failure: The gap sits in Southwark's most deprived north-central belt. Faraday ward records 31% child poverty. The compounding effect of transport poverty on residents who share protected characteristics has not been addressed in any published TfL or GLA equality impact assessment of the BLE scope decision.

The interim ask

Free Lime + Forest e-bike hire for verified residents — until Walworth Road reopens.

While the Mayor remains in non-compliance with the duties above, the GLA and TfL should fund free e-bike hire for residents of the gap area as substitutionary mobility provision. Specifically:

  • Who. Any resident with proof of address in SE17, SE5, SE1 or SE15 (postcodes covering the gap and Old Kent Road corridor). Verification via council tax bill, electoral register entry, or registered Oyster address — re-using existing TfL concession infrastructure.
  • What. A monthly free credit with Lime and Forest sufficient for daily commuting. Indicative: ~£30/month/resident (≈ 3 hours of e-bike time, or ~120 short hops). Operators already partner with TfL; the agreement template exists.
  • How long. Until Walworth Road station reopens to passenger service (Ask 01) — or until April 2030 — whichever comes first. A clear, time-bound, ratchet-able commitment.
  • How it's paid for. From the existing TfL active-travel and bus subsidy budgets. ~35,000 households × £30/month × 12 = ~£12.6m/year — about 0.25% of the BLE capital cost, and a fraction of the bus subsidy already paid for replacement service to the gap.

This is not radical. TfL already subsidises buses, the freedom pass, and the cycle hire scheme. Targeting active-travel subsidy at residents who have been denied rail provision is a coherent policy response — and an embarrassingly cheap one. We are also clear: this does not replace the rail demands. It pays the bill that's running while you build them.

Your rights

This isn't just bad luck. It's a duty being missed.

Greater London Authority Act 1999, s.141

The Mayor has a statutory duty to "develop and implement policies to promote and encourage safe, integrated, efficient and economic transport facilities and services to, from and within London."

Source: legislation.gov.uk

GLA Act 1999, s.142 — accessibility

The Mayor must produce a transport strategy including a timetable for providing transport accessible to people with mobility problems. "Accessible" starts with "exists at all".

Equality Act 2010, s.149 — Public Sector Equality Duty

TfL and the Mayor must have due regard to advancing equality of opportunity and fostering good relations between groups. The gap area is in the most deprived north-central belt of Southwark — Faraday ward has 31% of children in poverty. Transport poverty is a protected-characteristic issue.

Right to consultation

When the Mayor revises the Transport Strategy he must consult representative bodies. Residents' associations and constituted local groups can formally request to be consultees. We will help organise this.

UK petition thresholds

10,000 signatures = formal Government response. 100,000 = considered for parliamentary debate. The 2007 anti-road-pricing petition hit 1.8 million.

Judicial review (last resort)

If the Mayor or DfT made decisions that ignored the s.149 duty or failed to follow consultation rules, residents (with a community organisation as claimant and pro-bono legal support) could in principle bring a judicial review. Long, expensive, but it has worked elsewhere — e.g. challenges to road schemes. Not our first lever — but a real one.

A campaign like this can run on under £5,000/year (domain, email, print materials, occasional events) and scale to £20–50k for paid ads, leaflet drops and legal support. Five real options:

    Spacehive specialises in civic projects and councils sometimes match-fund. Crowdfunder is the largest UK platform. Both work well when paired with a clear target (e.g. "£3,000 for 50,000 leaflets across SE17/SE5/SE15").

    £630,000 pot, grants from £500 to £50,000. A constituted local group (or one with a sponsor) can apply for community-engagement, leaflet, event and design costs. Application portal: southwark.gov.uk.

  3. C Foundation for Integrated Transport

    Funded the £1m bus-campaign grant to Campaign for Better Transport. They like campaigns with clear policy asks and a credible coalition. We could partner with CBT or apply directly once we're constituted.

  4. D Local business sponsorship

    Old Kent Road developers (Berkeley, L&Q, Notting Hill Genesis) have hard commercial interest in better rail — homes sell faster. So do Burgess Park / East Street Market traders. Approach them for £500–£2,000 sponsor packages with logo placement on the campaign site.

  5. E Direct donations

    A "Buy us a leaflet" tier on Ko-fi or BuyMeACoffee — £3 for one street's letterbox drop. Low-friction, no platform fees beyond Stripe. Plug-and-play donation widget to be added once the campaign is constituted as a not-for-profit.

FAQ

Common questions.

Why ~85,000 residents and not the bigger 120k figure you sometimes see?

Earlier framings (including in the source video and in some media coverage) describe the "black hole" as Walworth + Camberwell + north Peckham + south Bermondsey, which adds up to roughly 120,000. But on close inspection, Peckham is well served by rail (Peckham Rye is a Thameslink + Windrush interchange; Queen's Road Peckham and Nunhead also serve the area), and most of Bermondsey has the Jubilee line plus London Bridge and Surrey Quays. The genuine, defensible "no station" desert is narrower: SE17 (Walworth) and the centre of SE5 (Camberwell), running into the Old Kent Road corridor. Adding Faraday (~11.7k), Newington (~14k), North Walworth (~14k), Camberwell Green (~12k), St Giles (~12k), and parts of Old Kent Road ward gets us to roughly 85,000. We've kept it tight on purpose — easy to defend in a meeting.

How did you estimate ~£300m of income tax?

~85,000 residents × ~60% working age (UK average) × ~70% employed ≈ 35,000 employed. Multiply by an average income tax + employee NI of ~£8,000 (assuming an effective average salary around £40k — well below Southwark's £58.4k median, which is appropriate for an area sitting in the borough's deprivation belt). That gives ~£280–300m. Add VAT, business rates and stamp duty and the real number is higher.

Why is reopening Walworth Road / Camberwell stations called "cheap"?

Because everything except the platforms and station boxes already exists. The Loughborough Junction → Elephant Thameslink line operates trains through both sites every few minutes today. The track, signalling, train fleet and operator are all already in place. TfL's 2014 study estimated the cost — including necessary platform rebuilds to modern accessibility standards — at well under £100m combined. For comparison: a single new tube station costs £500m+, and a new tube line costs billions. The 2014 figures need uplifting for inflation, but the order of magnitude is unchanged.

Isn't asking for a Bakerloo "west arm" unrealistic?

Possibly — in the short term. That's why it's ask 02, not 01. The cheap, achievable ask is reopening Walworth Rd and Camberwell. The west arm is the long-game ambition we want kept formally on the planning table rather than quietly struck out. It was on the official tube map in the 1940s. It deserves at least one properly costed feasibility study in this decade.

Why Brixton, not Denmark Hill, as the west-arm terminus?

Network economics. Denmark Hill has three suburban rail lines (Thameslink, Windrush, Southeastern) but no Underground. Brixton has the Victoria line — a tube. A Bakerloo terminus at Brixton would create the only new tube-to-tube interchange south of the Thames since the Jubilee extension. Tube-to-tube interchanges generate roughly 2–3× the ridership uplift of tube-to-rail because passengers can change Underground lines without leaving the network. The route Elephant → Walworth Rd → Camberwell Green → Loughborough Junction → Brixton also serves Loughborough Junction (currently no tube either), making it a four-stop win rather than a three-stop one. Denmark Hill is best served by reopening Camberwell station nearby (ask 01) rather than by detouring the tube line through it.

Isn't this just NIMBYs against new development?

The opposite. The campaign supports the 12,000+ new homes coming on Old Kent Road and the 25,000 the Bakerloo extension would unlock. It says: build them with the rail to serve them, and don't only build the rail on the eastern half of the gap. Right now Walworth and Camberwell are being asked to absorb new density and through-traffic without any new station.

Doesn't Camberwell already have Denmark Hill?

Denmark Hill is a station in Camberwell in the strict postal sense (it's SE5), but it sits at the far southern edge of the area, 10–18 minutes' walk from Camberwell Green and over 25 minutes from much of Walworth. It's already a busy interchange (Thameslink, Windrush, Southeastern) and is regularly cited as a capacity bottleneck. Adding 12,000 Old Kent Road homes to the east will load more passengers onto it. Treating Denmark Hill as "the Camberwell station" misreads both geography and capacity.

Hasn't the Bakerloo extension been promised forever?

Yes. A 1940s tube map showed it. 2014, 2017 and 2019 consultations all happened. Safeguarding was issued in 2021. What's missing is Treasury funding. The Mayor needs a multi-year capital settlement that explicitly names BLE. That's what we're pushing for.
